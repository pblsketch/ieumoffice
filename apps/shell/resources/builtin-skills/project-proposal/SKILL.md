# Writing a project proposal

Build a proposal that makes the decision easy for reviewers across Word, Hangul, HTML, or Markdown.

## 1. Clarify missing direction (at most once)

If target approver, budget/schedule constraints, or desired length are missing and materially affect the proposal, call `ask_questions` once (max 4 questions). Otherwise start immediately.

## 2. Proposal structure

1. **Executive Summary:** one-paragraph pitch stating the problem, proposed solution, and requested approval.
2. **Problem & Opportunity:** concrete pain points backed by supplied data or observations.
3. **Proposed Solution & Scope:** what is included and what is explicitly out of scope (Non-Goals).
4. **Execution Plan & Milestones:** phased schedule table (phase, deliverables, owner, target date).
5. **Expected Impact & Risks:** measurable outcomes plus realistic mitigations.
6. **Decision Ask:** clear next step required from the reader.

## 3. Writing discipline

- Separate facts from estimates clearly; mark assumptions honestly rather than fabricating metrics.
- Keep tables compact (4–6 columns max) and scannable.
- Match the document's language and formality consistently.

## 4. Check before you answer (required)

- Problem, solution, scope, schedule, risks, and decision ask are all present.
- Out-of-scope boundaries are stated clearly to prevent ambiguity.
- No placeholder text, invented statistics, or generic buzzword padding remains.
- Summarize the completed proposal and list any open items needing the user's numbers or sign-off.
